## The paper in five lines

- **Title:** "Harder Negatives, Cheaper Batches: Embedding-Clustered Training for Billion-Node Friend Retrieval". It was proposed 2026-09-30; the user has not confirmed or changed it, and the alternatives are listed in STORY.md.
- **Observation:** a model trained with random negatives already places graph neighbours close together. Clustering anchors by embedding therefore makes in-batch negatives harder and batches cheaper to encode (Sections 4, 6).
- **Exposure negatives** (suggestions shown and not added), the usual industry source of hard negatives, hurt. A slot-matched random control shows that the harm comes from which users they are (Section 5).
- **Caching the clustering signal** from training itself makes mining cheaper than random training (Section 6).
- **On skewed graphs,** concentrated batches cause regional forgetting, which a per-cluster row cap prevents; the production graph lacks that regime (Sections 7, 8). An online A/B of the deployed model completes the story (Section 6.5).
