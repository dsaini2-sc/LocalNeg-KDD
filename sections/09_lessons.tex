\section{Lessons for Practitioners}
\label{sec:lessons}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Do not pair an exposure with the user it was shown to.} On our graph that pairing costs 0.8\% of
        recall@1000, because the suggestion is as graph-close as a future friend. Given to other users, the same
        exposures help.
  \item \textbf{Look at the embeddings you already have.} A model trained with random negatives is graph-local, so
        clustering on its embeddings gives hard in-batch negatives and cheaper batches at once.
  \item \textbf{Cache the clustering signal.} The training loop already computes the embeddings the scheduler needs;
        re-embedding for mining costs more than clustered batching saves.
  \item \textbf{Measure batch concentration before training, and cap it.} The worst-batch same-cluster pair rate of the
        uncapped plan separates graphs that collapse from graphs that do not. The cap prevents the collapse where it
        matters and costs nothing where it does not, so it is safe to leave on.
\end{itemize}
