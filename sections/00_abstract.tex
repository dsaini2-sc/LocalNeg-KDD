\begin{abstract}
Embedding-based friend retrieval on a billion-node social graph is trained contrastively, and its negatives decide both
what the model learns and what training costs. The conventional industrial source of hard negatives, logged exposures,
hurts on this graph: adding them lowers macro recall@1000 by 0.35 points, while a slot-matched random control costs
nothing, so the harm lies in which users are exposed. We start instead from the model production already trains. Its
embeddings, learned with random negatives only, are graph-local: one-hop neighbours average a cosine similarity of 0.70
with a user, random users 0.07. Clustering anchors on these embeddings therefore yields hard in-batch negatives and
batches that sample 35\% fewer nodes. With the clustering signal cached from training itself, clustered batching raises
macro recall@1000 by 1.6--1.7 points at full scale while using 7\% fewer GPU-hours than random batching, and the
deployed model increased new friends made with bidirectional communication by 2.09\% in an online A/B test. The same
concentration can fail: on Collab, Citation2 and LiveJournal, uncapped clustered batches collapse onto single clusters
and end 8--34 points of recall@1000 below random training. A per-cluster row cap prevents the collapse in every seed,
and the concentration of the uncapped plan shows in advance whether a graph needs it.
\end{abstract}
