\section{Mining Negatives from Embedding Clusters}
\label{sec:method}

Section~\ref{sec:locality} shows that users who are close in embedding space are close in the graph, and that a
user's graph neighbours look like positives to the model. Clustered batching turns both facts into a training signal:
if a batch is built from a few clusters of similar users, every anchor's in-batch negatives come from its own region,
and the batch is cheaper to encode.

\subsection{Clustered-anchor batching}
\label{sec:method:batching}

Training proceeds in windows of 50 updates. At the start of a window, each worker takes the positive rows it will
train on in that window (102{,}400 rows at 2{,}048 rows per batch), clusters their distinct anchors by query
embedding into balanced clusters of about 64 anchors with recursive two-means, and packs the clusters into batches
following NGAME's batch ordering~\cite{dahiya2023ngame}, targeting 16 clusters per batch. All rows of an anchor stay
in its cluster, so an anchor's positives train together. Random negatives are kept (512 per worker, 8{,}192 per step),
as is the frequency correction of the loss. The first 200 updates of every run use random batches; clustering starts
from that shared prefix model, and every arm, random included, continues from the same prefix checkpoint.

\subsection{A cached clustering signal}
\label{sec:method:cache}

Clustering needs a current query embedding for every anchor of the next window. Computing them fresh means an
inference pass over the window's anchors every 50 updates, on the GPU and on the critical path. We instead keep a
cache of query embeddings that the training loop fills as a by-product: every training step already computes the
query embeddings of its anchors, and we store them. A CPU thread clusters the next window from a snapshot of the
cache while the GPU trains on the current one, so gradients always use current embeddings and only the batch
assignment uses cached ones. A single inference pass with the prefix model fills the cache before the first clustered
window.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:method:cost}

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Training cost at full scale over the same 15{,}479 updates (16 A100 GPUs, one seed). Mining and bootstrap
  inference are charged to the method; checkpoint I/O and evaluation are excluded.}
  \label{tab:cost}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    & Random & Fresh clustered & Cached clustered \\
    \midrule
    Seconds per update, training steps only & 4.74 & 4.18 & 4.04 \\
    Exposed mining (s, whole run) & 23 & 18{,}200 & 47--57 \\
    Bootstrap inference (s) & 0 & 0 & 5{,}641 \\
    Total GPU-hours & 326 & 369 & 303 \\
    Relative to random & -- & +13.0\% & $-$7.0\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Because clustered batches are cheaper to encode (Section~\ref{sec:locality:cost}), clustered training steps are
12--15\% faster than random ones: 4.74\,s per update for random batches, 4.18\,s for fresh clustering and 4.04\,s for
cached clustering (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Fresh clustering gives the saving back and more: its inference pass costs
54\,s every 50 updates, 22\% of its training loop, so it costs 13\% more GPU time than random training. The cached
version exposes 47--57\,s of planning over the whole run, 0.1\% of loop time, and after charging it the one-time
bootstrap pass of 5{,}641\,s, it costs 7\% less GPU time than random training, 15\% less without the bootstrap. The
bootstrap is 8\% of a three-epoch run and amortises with longer training. Full-state checkpoints are larger, because
the 2.7\,GB per-worker cache is part of the resumable state (95\,s vs.\ 37\,s per save).
These costs are from one seed.

\subsection{Retrieval quality on the production graph}
\label{sec:method:results}

  \caption{Macro recall@1000 (\%) on the frozen production panel (38.96M queries, 85.3M candidates), mean $\pm$ s.d.\

  \label{tab:internal}
  \small

    Fresh clustered, capped & 34.06 $\pm$ 0.05 (2) & 35.74 $\pm$ 0.01 (2) & 36.53 (1) \\
    Cached clustered, capped & 33.84 $\pm$ 0.05 (2) & 35.74 $\pm$ 0.00 (2) & 36.44 (1) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Clustered batching improves retrieval at every point of training (Table~\ref{tab:internal}). After three epochs,
random training reaches 34.79\% macro recall@1000 and clustered training 36.38--36.53\%, a gain of 1.6--1.7 points,
or 4.6--5.0\% relative. The gain is present from the first epoch (1.7--2.1 points) and does not close as training
continues. The cached clustering signal costs almost nothing in quality: cached and fresh clustering differ by at most
0.22 points at any point. Capping, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:capping}, is neutral on this graph, within 0.2 points
of the uncapped arm at every point. The seeds agree closely: at two epochs every arm's standard deviation is at most
0.06 points, well below the gap to random. We report the final model of every run; no checkpoint is selected.
