\section{Exposure Negatives Hurt}
\label{sec:exposure}

\head{The conventional source} An exposure negative is a user who was shown to the source as a friend suggestion
during the label window and was not added. Exposures are the standard industrial source of hard
negatives~\cite{huang2020ebr}. They are free to log, they come from the production candidate distribution, and a user
who saw a suggestion and passed on it looks like a genuine no. Our data preparation stores one exposure per source, the
most-shown suggestion that was not added, 59.5M pairs in total. The historical trainer used them as extra candidates in
the contrastive loss, next to the in-batch and random negatives.

Exposures are as hard as negatives get on this graph. We took 4{,}253 panel users and their stored exposures, in the
query space of the random-trained models of Section~\ref{sec:locality}. The exposure's cosine similarity to the user
averages 0.69, against 0.61 for the users' own future friends and 0.70 for their one-hop neighbours. Compared user by
user, the exposure is 0.05 closer than the same user's future friends. Most exposures are friends of friends: 59\% lie
exactly two hops away, where the similarity averages 0.77. To the model, an exposure looks like a positive. That makes
it a hard negative. It also suggests how it could hurt: pushing a user away from users that look like its positives can
push it away from the positives as well, which is what we find below.

\head{Effect} Adding the stored exposures back to an otherwise identical training run lowers macro recall@1000 from
34.07\% to 33.79\%, a relative loss of 0.8\%. Every cutoff and ranking metric falls with it (Table~\ref{tab:exposure}).
The production recipe had already dropped exposure negatives, after removing them gained 1.07\% relative recall@1000
on an older snapshot of the graph. A variant that also dropped them before sampling gained 0.93\%. The harm sits where
the exposures are. In a 1-in-1{,}000 sample of queries, those that own an exposure label lose 0.72 points in the
add-back model, and the others gain 0.04.
\evidence{exposure-ablation/READ-three-seeds.md; HANDOFF-EXPOSURE-NEGATIVES-2026-09-27.md; prism-evaluation/PLATFORM-CHECK.md}

\head{Where the harm comes from} An exposure negative does three things at once. It is sampled and encoded, and counted
in the frequency correction. It is a negative for its own source. And, like every candidate in the batch, it is a
negative for the batch's other queries. Four more arms separate these (Table~\ref{tab:exposure}). Filling the slots
with random users changes nothing, so extra negative pressure is not the cause. Encoding the exposures without using
them as negatives changes nothing either. Making each exposure a negative for its own source only costs 0.49 points,
more than the full recipe. Using it also as a negative for the other queries gives back 0.20. Finally, we give each source
another source's exposure instead of its own. The loss turns into a gain: 0.43 points of recall@1000 over no exposure
negatives, with gains at every cutoff. The whole harm lies in pairing each exposure with the user it was shown to.

\head{Why the pairing hurts} A stored exposure is as close to its source in the graph as a future friend is. Half of
the pairs share a mutual friend, and they average 4.1 common friends; (query, future friend) pairs share one 36\% of
the time and average 4.3. Shuffled pairs almost never share one (0.08\%). Penalising a user against such a suggestion
teaches the model that graph closeness is not evidence of friendship, the very signal retrieval relies on. The loss
follows graph closeness accordingly. In a sample of 1.22M future friendships, the own-source penalty costs 1.8 to 1.9
points of recall@1000 for future friends with 2 to 9 mutual friends, and 0.4 for those with none. At recall@10, future
friends with no mutual friend lose nothing. Few exposures are positives in disguise: 0.51\% of stored exposure pairs
coincide with requests accepted within the training window, and another 0.51\% are accepted in the following seven
days. Given to other users, the same exposures are hard negatives without that contradiction, and they help.
\evidence{exposure-ablation/why/proximity.py, PROXIMITY-RANKS.md; LABEL-AUDIT.md}
