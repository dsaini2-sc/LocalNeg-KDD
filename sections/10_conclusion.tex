\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A retrieval model trained with random negatives already organises users by graph neighbourhood. Clustering on its own
embeddings turns that organisation into hard negatives and cheaper training. On a billion-node friend graph, cached
clustered batching improves macro recall@1000 by 1.6 points at 9--10\% lower training cost. The deployed model
improved new friends made with bidirectional communication by 2.09\% online. The conventional alternative, exposure
negatives, hurts. The same concentration that makes clustered batches useful makes models forget other regions when
positive supply is skewed. A per-cluster row cap prevents it on three public graphs, and a simple concentration
statistic tells when it is needed.
