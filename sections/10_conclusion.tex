\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A retrieval model trained with random negatives already organises users by graph neighbourhood, and clustering on
its own embeddings turns that organisation into hard negatives and cheaper training. On a billion-node friend graph,
cached clustered batching improves macro recall@1000 by 1.6 to 1.7 points at 7\% lower training cost, the deployed
model improved new friends made with bidirectional communication by 2.09\% online, and the conventional alternative,
exposure negatives, hurts. The same concentration that makes clustered batches useful makes models forget other regions
when positive supply is skewed; a per-cluster row cap prevents it on three public graphs, and a simple concentration
statistic tells when it is needed.
