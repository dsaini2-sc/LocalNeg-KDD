\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Negatives for embedding-based retrieval.}
Two-tower retrieval models are commonly trained with in-batch and sampled negatives, corrected for sampling frequency
with a logQ term~\cite{yi2019sampling}. Harder negatives come from two places. Facebook's embedding-based search found
that training on non-click impressions instead of random negatives cost 55\% absolute recall for people search, which it
attributes to impressions over-representing hard cases, and that mined hard negatives help only when blended with random
ones~\cite{huang2020ebr}. Dense text retrieval mines
them from an approximate-nearest-neighbour index that is periodically rebuilt~\cite{xiong2021ance,karpukhin2020dpr} and
has to filter the false negatives such mining brings in~\cite{qu2021rocketqa}. PinSage draws hard negatives from
personalised-PageRank neighbourhoods with a curriculum~\cite{ying2018graph}.
Our exposure result is the blended case on a friend graph: exposures added to random negatives still hurt, and a
slot-matched random control shows that the harm lies in which users they are, not in the added negatives.

\paragraph{Batch construction as negative mining.}
Composing batches so that in-batch negatives are hard is an established alternative to mining them per example.
TAS-Balanced clusters queries by topic and samples each batch from one cluster~\cite{hofstatter2021tasb}. NGAME, in
extreme classification, periodically clusters the training points on their current embeddings into equal-size clusters
and builds each mini-batch from a few random clusters, so that in-batch negatives are hard without a mining
index~\cite{dahiya2023ngame}; our scheduler builds on this batch ordering. At every refresh NGAME re-embeds all
training points with the current encoder, and because its clusters are balanced in data points, nothing bounds how
many positive rows one cluster contributes to a batch. ANCE-Tele observes that
refreshing hard negatives makes dense retrievers forget and counters it with negatives carried over from earlier
iterations~\cite{sun2022ancetele}. We add three things these works do not address: on a graph, embedding clusters are
graph regions, so clustered batches are also cheaper to encode; the clustering signal can be cached from training itself instead of re-embedded;
and batch concentration is a failure mode of its own, regional forgetting, which a per-cluster cap prevents and a
concentration statistic predicts.

\paragraph{Graph locality and training efficiency.}
Sampling-based graph neural networks encode each node from a sampled neighbourhood~\cite{hamilton2017inductive,ying2018graph},
so the cost of a batch depends on how much its neighbourhoods overlap. Cluster-GCN exploits this by building batches
from graph partitions~\cite{chiang2019clustergcn}. Clustered-anchor batching obtains a similar locality without a
partitioner, from the model's own embeddings, and applies it to supervision rows rather than nodes. The regional
forgetting we observe is an instance of catastrophic interference under non-stationary training
data~\cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic}, here created by the batch scheduler.
