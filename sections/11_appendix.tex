\subsection{Evaluation panel}
The panel's queries are the users with at least one accepted friend request in the three days after the training window, and their
positives are those requests. Candidates are a fixed uniform hash sample of 50M users together with every positive target, 85.3M users in
all. Ranking is exact over the candidates. Macro recall@$k$ is the per-query share of positives ranked in the top $k$, averaged over
queries.

\subsection{Cost of index-based mining}
\label{app:ance}
We estimate what ANCE-style mining~\cite{xiong2021ance} would cost on the production graph, from measured components only; we did not
run it. Batches are random, each training row gets one negative mined from a nearest-neighbour index of the current model, and the index
over all 966M users is refreshed once per epoch. Each refresh re-embeds every user and the 104M training sources at the rate the cache
bootstrap measured, 16{,}632 users per second on 16 A100s: 858 GPU-hours over three refreshes. One extra encoded negative per row makes each step about 27\% slower. Adding the stored exposure negative, present for 57\% of
sources, raised trainer wall time by 15.6\%, and a linear model of step time in sampled nodes fitted to the four full-scale arms gives
14--31\% for the footprint of one negative. The steps therefore cost about 419 GPU-hours against random batching's 330, and the batches
are random, so none of the clustered-step saving applies. In total that is about 1{,}280 GPU-hours, 3.9 times random training;
\ours{} uses 301, 0.91 times. Index build,
search and hosting are excluded, and more frequent refreshes, as ANCE uses, raise the re-embedding cost proportionally.

\subsection{Cap feasibility}
A window whose largest cluster holds more rows than the cap can place across the window's batches cannot be scheduled exactly. The
scheduler used in every capped run of this paper stops with an error on such a window instead of relaxing the cap. The one exception is
a final window shorter than the refresh interval, which is scheduled with an exact equal-batch order that still respects the cap. Every
capped run reported here completed, so every window was scheduled under the strict cap.
